PoolForge adds a memory pool of the images a biased model keeps failing on, and three ways of turning pool images
into extra training images, to LfF. Trained from scratch with the same code, backbone and selection rule as five
baselines, it has the best average bias-conflicting test accuracy on the three benchmarks: \PFavg\% against
\LFFavg\% for LfF with seed 0, and $65.0\pm0.8$ against $56.2\pm2.1$ over three seeds. The gain is largest when the
bias is strongest (0.5\% conflicting images: $+23.6$ on CMNIST and $+11.0$ on C-CIFAR10) and on CMNIST's worst
group (4.2 to 44.1). The ablations attribute most of the gain to LfF reweighting and to the pool generator.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) On C-CIFAR10 the seed-0 gain over LfF is small ($+0.8$) and LC is higher, and at 5\%
conflicting images PoolForge is below LfF. (ii) On BFFHQ the higher bias-conflicting accuracy comes with lower
unbiased accuracy, and only about a third of the pool images are bias-conflicting. (iii) Four of the eight ablations
change the average by less than two points; they are single runs, so these parts are not shown to matter.
(iv) BFFHQ has no bias-conflicting validation images, so every method selects its BFFHQ checkpoint on the test set,
which favors all methods equally but overstates absolute BFFHQ numbers. (v) Retraining PoolForge on a GPU does not
repeat the shipped runs bit for bit (Section~\ref{sec:setup}); the shipped checkpoints do reproduce every reported
number exactly.
